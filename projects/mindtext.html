MindText

An HCI project to make journaling feel like texting a friend. MindText uses an iMessage inspired interface, short AI prompts, and summaries to keep people writing without feeling overwhelmed.

Project snapshot

Role: researcher, designer, and developer. Goal: reduce journaling friction by mirroring the cadence of messaging apps. Built in a standalone Next.js app with chat and summaries views.

Research approach

Started with a needs and requirements analysis: structured interviews + 125 App Store reviews (five journaling apps) coded for themes like simplicity, privacy, and prompts. Findings shaped the initial features: chat-style entry, lightweight prompts, summaries + search, and ad-free experience.

User study 1 — paper prototypes

Compared two low fidelity prototypes with four participants: Design A (chat-first) vs Design B (summary-first). I simulated AI replies live. Most participants favored the chat version because it felt faster and simpler; “interface felt natural” and “easy to understand” scored higher for Design A. Confusions were around where to start writing (B) and saving an entry (also B).

Key takeaways: keep the chat-first path, add clearer save feedback, and add a quick search entry point.

User study 2 — functional prototype

Ran the working Next.js build with four participants. Tasks: respond to a prompt, observe pacing, then gather post-task ratings (ease of starting, naturalness, openness). Everyone wrote faster than in Study 1, needed no UI explanation, and sped up after the first AI reply.

Ratings (1–10): ease of starting 6–9, naturalness 7–9, openness 6–8. Comments highlighted momentum (“prompts kept me going”) and authenticity (“felt more real than the mock”)

What I learned

  • The conversational model works: once a reply appears, people continue writing.
  • Hesitation drops when the UI simulates texting patterns. No instruction needed.
  • Next steps: add voice input and richer summaries.
