APP-05

MindText

An HCI project that makes journaling feel like texting a friend, using an iMessage-style interface, short AI prompts, and entry summaries.

Role
Researcher, designer, developer
Type
HCI research project
Built with
Next.js
Studies
Two rounds, four participants each

The idea

MindText works like a messaging app. You answer a short prompt, it replies, and your entries get grouped into summaries so you can look back at themes later.

Research

I started with a needs analysis, using structured interviews and 125 App Store reviews across five journaling apps, coded for themes like simplicity, privacy, and prompts. Those findings shaped the first set of features (chat-style entry, light prompts, summaries with search, and no ads).

Study 1: paper prototypes

Four participants compared two low-fidelity prototypes: Design A (chat first) and Design B (summary first). I played the AI and wrote its replies live. Most preferred the chat version because it felt faster and simpler, and it scored higher on “interface felt natural” and “easy to understand.” The confusion in Design B was about where to start writing and how to save an entry.

Changes after this round were to keep the chat-first flow, make saving clearer, and add a quicker way to get to search.

Study 2: working prototype

Four participants used the working Next.js build. They responded to a prompt, kept going at their own pace, and then rated the experience. Everyone wrote faster than in the first study, nobody needed the UI explained, and people sped up after the first AI reply.

Ratings out of 10: ease of starting 6 to 9, naturalness 7 to 9, openness 6 to 8. The comments were about momentum (“prompts kept me going”) and authenticity (“felt more real than the mock”).

What I learned

  • People kept writing once the AI replied, which supported the chat-first design.
  • Participants hesitated less when the UI behaved like texting, and nobody needed instructions.
  • Next steps would be voice input and richer summaries.
